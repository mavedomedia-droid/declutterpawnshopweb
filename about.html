The problem

Nigeria already sells secondhand. Trust is what's been missing.

Every day, phones, laptops, sofas, and clothing move between people on Instagram, WhatsApp, and word of mouth. The behaviour is there. What's been missing is structure: payments both sides trust, a way to verify that what you listed is what you deliver, and a clean handoff when they meet.

Instagram sellers deal with buyers who ghost after payment. Buyers deal with sellers who deliver something different from the photos. Both sides deal with "send the money first" and hope for the best. The trust layer was missing. That's what DPS is.

Escrow protection

An escrow system that actually works.

Payment confirms the order, but neither side has to trust the other. The money sits in escrow -- not with the seller -- until the exchange is done. A PIN is exchanged at pickup to confirm the item matches. That's the moment funds release.

The PIN is your last checkpoint.You're not confirming the sale when you pay. You're confirming it after you've held the item in your hands and decided it matches what you agreed to buy.
1
Buyer pays into escrow
Paystack holds the money. Order is confirmed. Nobody touches it yet.
2
Contacts shared both ways
Buyer and seller get each other's details. Pickup arranged directly.
3
Inspect in person

4
PIN confirms and releases
