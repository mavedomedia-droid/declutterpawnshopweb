What we're building

Decluttering is already a habit. We're making it work better.

Nigerians already sell phones, furniture, clothes and electronics every day, on WhatsApp, on Instagram, anywhere people are listening. The behaviour is there. The problem is everything around it: awkward payments, strangers who ghost you, no structure, and no trust on either side.

The goal is simple. Turn idle items into cash, free up space, and do it safely. In this economy that matters. Something sitting in your house doing nothing is someone else's need and your extra income.

Why it's safe

Trust was the missing piece.

The problem with selling pre-owned in Nigeria was never demand. It was trust. Buyers worried about paying and not getting what was described. Sellers worried about being scammed or shortchanged.

01
Escrow sits in the middle.
The buyer's money is held from the moment they pay. The seller cannot reach it until the handover is confirmed. Neither side is asked to go first.
02
A PIN only the buyer has.
On payment the buyer gets a 4 digit PIN by email. The seller is paid when the buyer hands it over at pickup, which only happens if they are happy with the item.
03
Walking away is allowed.
Item not as described? Keep the PIN. The money has not left DPS, so you are refunded. That is the whole point of holding it.

Haggling, properly

Think it's too much? Make an offer.

Every listing takes offers. There is a ₦300 fee, which exists to keep the silly ones away, and the seller has 24 hours to accept, counter or decline. You can withdraw while it is still open.

Sellers can mark an item negotiable when they list it, which tells buyers an offer is genuinely welcome rather than a waste of everyone's time.
